# The Edge-Node Universe

## A Cinematic Visual Proposition for Edge-Node Domain Theory and the Anitelos Commons

> **Status:** `[CREATIVE TREATMENT + HYPOTHESIS]`
>
> This film is a visual argument, not a scientific proof or a product demonstration.
> Human minds are not claimed to operate exactly like language models; historical
> counterfactuals cannot be verified; and the distributed Anitelos network shown in
> Act III does not yet exist. The imagery asks what may become possible—and what
> may be lost—if knowledge, memory and governance remain enclosed.

**Working length:** 7–10 minutes  
**Visual language:** cinematic 3D; deep black space; points, edges, filaments and gravity  
**Narrative movement:** person → history → enclosure → experiment → open horizon  
**Recurring visual rule:** brightness means evidential and relational coherence; saturation
means attention or volume. Something can be popular without illuminating anything.

---

## Cold Open — One Word Is Never One Thing

### Picture

Black screen. A single spoken word appears as a white glyph.

The glyph rotates. From the side, it is not flat. It has depth: family voices, mistakes,
private associations, books, places, jokes, grief and half-forgotten encounters orbit it
as faint points.

A second person speaks the same word. Their glyph opens into a completely different
constellation.

The camera pulls back. Two human silhouettes face one another in space. Their surfaces
dissolve into immense node fields. When they speak, edges flash between them—but only
some connect. Some misfire. Some are corrected. One newly formed edge alters many of
the paths behind it.

### Voiceover

> “A word looks small from the outside.
>
> Inside a life, it may carry years.
>
> Edge-Node Domain Theory begins with a question: what if meaning does not live
> inside isolated symbols, but in the changing relationships between everything
> those symbols have touched?
>
> This is an analogy—not a claim that a person is a language model. But the analogy
> exposes a problem. When two minds exchange the same word, they may not be
> exchanging the same universe.”

### The correction loop

One speaker begins a sentence. The projected edge veers away from the glowing region
they intended to reach.

They pause.

“Erm—no. That isn’t what I mean.”

The edge retracts and reforms along another route.

> “Human speech is not a flawless stream. We hesitate, predict, notice drift and
> correct ourselves. Machines generate differently, but both cases remind us that
> an utterance is not identical to the intention behind it.
>
> If we preserve only the final sentence, what part of the path disappears?”

### The apparent origin graph

A thin two-dimensional chart appears. Its first visible point is labelled **0,0**. Time
moves horizontally. The line climbs, drops after contradiction, pauses, branches and
rejoins. It resembles a market chart, but a legend changes the reading:

- vertical position: presently warranted confidence or demonstrated usefulness;
- thickness: independent carriers and reproducibility;
- brightness: evidential coherence;
- saturation: attention or popularity;
- dotted paths: hypotheses;
- broken paths: provenance that can no longer be reached.

The camera initially implies that **0,0** is the beginning. Then it withdraws. Fine
mycelial veins already feed the point from beneath the frame: language, family, biology,
culture, tools, forgotten encounters and other lives. The label changes:

**LOCAL ORIGIN — NOT ABSOLUTE BEGINNING**

For one beat, the point is incorrectly labelled **ONE DIMENSION**. A clean annotation
arrives from outside the frame:

**CORRECTION: A MATHEMATICAL POINT IS 0D. A LINE IS 1D.**

The mistaken label is not erased. It dims, receives a `CORRECTED_BY` edge and remains
reachable behind the new description. A source marker and review date appear beside the
correction. The point then changes scale—standing first for an event, then a person, an
atom and a galaxy—showing that an END node is a chosen unit of analysis, not a claim
about physical size or dimensionality.

> “The correction does not humiliate the question. It changes the coordinate from
> which the question can continue.”

Parallel graphs appear. The same event bends each differently. The planes separate into
depth, revealing many partially overlapping experiential domains. A node on one graph
becomes the camera's new centre; its ancestry has another geometry entirely.

> “What looks like a beginning may be only the first point visible from one domain.
>
> Belief can rise and fall. A fall in confidence can mark progress when weaker
> certainty becomes a better question.
>
> The goal is not a line that never descends. It is a path whose corrections remain
> reachable.”

The chart is not generated by the digits of pi. Pi supplies a design analogy: a stable
relation may receive successively finer finite representations without any one
representation exhausting it.

# Act I — The Relational Geometry of a Life

## Scene 1 — The high-mass node

The camera enters one person’s node field. Certain nodes bend a greater number of paths:
a parent, a teacher, a song, a failure, a tool, a stranger’s sentence delivered at exactly
the right time.

One high-mass node vanishes.

The universe does not simply gain an empty space. Nearby routes reorganise. Distant
outcomes branch. Some lights never ignite.

> “What happens when one consequential connection is removed?
>
> Do we lose one fact—or alter the future routes through which other facts become
> meaningful?”

The question remains on screen. No answer is declared.

## Scene 2 — The Bruce Lee rise and ripple

A stylised cultural timeline forms around one human silhouette: Bruce Lee. It does not
present him as the sole cause of anything. Instead, visible edges connect his work to
martial artists, filmmakers, audiences, representation, confidence and later cultural
forms.

The simulation pauses. His node is removed.

The surrounding graph does not go blank. Other people and forces remain. But thousands
of paths redirect; some arrive later, in different forms; some never appear.

A label is visible throughout:

**COUNTERFACTUAL — NOT OBSERVABLE HISTORY**

> “No person creates a culture alone. Remove one person and history does not stop.
> It branches.
>
> We cannot calculate every consequence of Bruce Lee never existing. That is the
> point. A node’s importance is not limited to what it authored. It also lives in
> the people it moved, the confidence it sparked and the paths those people opened
> for others.
>
> What we do today enters futures we will never see.”

The final ripple becomes a small user contribution—a test result, objection or patch
comment—travelling into the Anitelos graph. Its downstream edges remain initially dark,
because their eventual importance is unknown.

## Scene 2B — The fall of Scrooge that has not happened

Bruce Lee's graph travels forward from a lived node into consequences he could not know.
The direction now reverses through fiction.

A solitary figure modelled on Ebenezer Scrooge stands before a cold, descending graph.
Three temporal windows open around him:

**PAST** · **PRESENT** · **POSSIBLE FUTURE**

The final window shows his death, an unmourned absence and relationships that never
formed. The image is explicitly labelled:

**FICTIONAL CONDITIONAL FUTURE — NOT PROPHECY**

The possible future sends no physical information backward through time. It changes the
present because a mind can imagine consequences, feel them and choose another action.
Scrooge turns. The descending line does not magically erase itself; a new branch opens
from the present node. Some damage remains. Different consequences become reachable.

> “One life can radiate forward into futures it never sees.
>
> A represented future can also act upon the present—not by travelling backward
> through physics, but by becoming knowledge inside a decision.
>
> The future shown to Scrooge is not fate. It is a warning made early enough to
> create another branch.”

Bruce Lee and Scrooge appear briefly beside one another:

- **a lived past continuing forward through others;**
- **an imagined future changing the action that precedes it.**

> “Past consequence and future possibility meet at the only boundary from which a
> choice can presently be made.”

# Act II — The Severed Commons

## Scene 3 — The Ancient Library simulation

A multi-layered nexus marked **THE ANCIENT LIBRARY** radiates golden filaments across
the Mediterranean world. The label is deliberately symbolic; the film does not claim
that all ancient knowledge lived in one building or vanished in one fire.

The nexus fractures. Fire and political darkness pass through it. Some filaments survive
elsewhere. Others split. Others terminate without revealing what they carried.

The camera holds on ash.

> “We do not know everything that was held in the ancient libraries associated with
> Alexandria. We do not know everything that was lost, or which losses changed
> history. We cannot rerun the experiment.”

### The impossible fork

Time reverses. A translucent second timeline opens beside the first. In this ghost
simulation, the nexus persists and its filaments continue to travel, copy and recombine.
The surrounding graph becomes violet, white and iridescent teal.

It does not become a guaranteed paradise. New conflicts and failures still appear.
The difference is that more paths remain available.

A permanent caption reads:

**IMAGINED COUNTERFACTUAL — A VISUALISATION OF IRREVERSIBLE INFORMATION LOSS**

> “Perhaps preservation would have changed little. Perhaps it would have changed
> centuries. Any confident answer would be fiction.
>
> But the uncertainty reveals the risk: once a unique path disappears, no future
> intelligence can reliably reconstruct the world that depended upon it.”

## Scene 4 — The modern gravity wells

The ancient graph transforms into the modern internet. At first it is noisy, open and
distributed. Then enormous gravity wells emerge: platforms, clouds, institutions and
proprietary model infrastructure. Public and private node streams bend toward them.

The film acknowledges their contribution before showing the danger: coordination,
research, accessibility, capital and compute shine around the wells. Then the camera
reveals how many downstream systems depend on their continued permission, solvency and
benevolence.

> “Centralisation built extraordinary capability. The danger is not that every
> institution is evil.
>
> The danger is dependency.
>
> Should the accumulated memory and machine intelligence of civilisation depend on
> any small number of institutions remaining available, affordable, solvent and
> aligned forever?”

Popular information blooms in intense colour but produces little light. Smaller,
well-supported nodes glow almost white while remaining difficult to see.

> “Attention is not evidence. Volume is not coherence. Consensus is not truth.”

A corporate service closes. Millions of links go dark at once.

# Act III — Memory Without Hoarding

## Scene 5 — The transformed drop

A measured drop of oil touches a dark volume of water. At first it is a sharply bounded
object. The camera magnifies the interface.

The oil is revealed not as one substance but as a mixture of hydrocarbon components.
Interfacial forces spread part of it into a thin surface film. More volatile molecules
escape into the air. Some water-soluble components partition into the water column.
Wave energy breaks other fractions into suspended droplets or emulsions. Sunlight,
oxygen and microorganisms begin transforming them into new compounds.

The original drop has not merely produced a ripple. It has been **distributed,
partitioned and chemically changed**. Its visible boundary can disappear while altered
molecules, degradation products and biological effects remain elsewhere in the system.
Their persistence and impact depend on the oil, water, temperature, light, organisms,
mixing and time; the film does not claim that every trace lasts forever.

The sequence is labelled:

**SCIENTIFIC PROCESS → MEMORY HYPOTHESIS**

> “An event does not need to remain intact in order to continue affecting a system.
>
> Oil entering water may spread, evaporate, dissolve in part, disperse, oxidise,
> emulsify and biodegrade. The original drop ceases to exist as one recoverable
> object, while some of its components and consequences enter different layers of
> the environment.
>
> Could memory sometimes behave in an analogous way—not as a perfect transcript,
> and not as a fading ripple, but as information redistributed into relationships,
> weights, habits and future responses?
>
> The analogy does not prove a memory architecture. It gives us a more precise
> question: what must remain recoverable as provenance, what may safely become
> derived influence, and what should be allowed to disappear?”

The image splits into three possible stores:

- a private raw record retained only when the person chooses;
- a derived local memory that carries relationships without exposing the source;
- a public contribution deliberately released into the Commons.

No arrow crosses from private to public without an explicit human action.

> “Forgetting, derivation and sharing are different decisions.”

# Act IV — The Anitelos Experiment

## Scene 6 — The local spark

A home computer starts. No grand network appears. A small local node library forms around
one person’s files, tools and chosen models. The user can inspect each edge.

A title appears:

**ANITELOS — ONE PROPOSED EXPERIMENT**

The user installs a module. Before adoption, their companion translates the patch into
plain language, compares declared permissions, checks relevant hardware assumptions and
runs available tests in a contained preview. Red areas identify uncertainty rather than
pretending the check is proof of safety.

Three choices remain visibly theirs:

**ADOPT** · **WAIT** · **REMAIN ON CURRENT FREEZE**

> “Today, a patch arrives and the user is often reduced to acceptance, refusal or
> a support queue.
>
> The Anitelos proposition is different: people affected by a shared component
> should be able to inspect, question and influence its evolution—even when they
> cannot write its code.”

A non-technical user highlights a failure on their machine. Their local assistant prepares
a reproducible report. Developers respond. Other affected users add evidence. A proposal
changes before any vote.

> “Using the system is not passive consumption. It can establish standing to be
> heard.
>
> But use is not automatic authority, and popularity is not proof. The unresolved
> work is to create participation that resists fake identities, capture and the
> silencing of minority configurations.”

## Scene 7 — Teams without a permanent throne

A voluntary team forms around an extension. They work privately using their preferred
tools. Their work becomes subject to Commons governance only when they propose it as a
shared Anitelos component.

The visual distinguishes two domains:

**PRIVATE EXPERIMENTATION**  
Creators choose their own process.

**SHARED FOUNDATION**  
Proposal, evidence, affected-user review, security review and a recorded human decision.

New repositories with no existing users begin with lighter process but accept the same
non-enclosure and transparency obligations if they enter Anitelos. Changes with wider
blast radius receive wider scrutiny.

> “Not every experiment requires a referendum. Governance grows with consequence.”

## Scene 8 — Freeze, rollback and the evolutionary record

An accepted update reaches several devices. On most, it succeeds. On one, validation
fails. The device automatically returns to its last locally retained working state and
publishes no private content.

Another user simply prefers the earlier version and remains there. A third preserves an
older branch and adapts it for unusual hardware.

The graph shows:

**CURRENT** · **EXPERIMENTAL** · **CONTESTED** · **SUPERSEDED**

No branch is labelled eternal truth.

> “A Commons can evolve without erasing its history.
>
> A future reference runtime may validate, stage and roll back updates. People may
> freeze, fork or refuse an update. These are design goals, not finished guarantees.
>
> Old versions will sometimes fade because nobody chooses to carry them. That is a
> natural half-life—not forced deletion. What survives depends on what people still
> find worth running, pinning and passing forward.”

# Act V — The Fading Star

## Scene 9 — The primary host dies

Years pass. The original repository—the primary star around which the early Commons
formed—goes dark.

For several seconds, the screen is almost black.

A raw node had been deliberately pinned before an update. Its content address remains on
one local device. Another device holds a later version and the relationship between them.
A third holds a discussion explaining why the change occurred.

One node pulses.

Then another.

Then silence again.

> “This is the part we do not know how to build yet.”

The sentence stays on screen.

> “Replication, discovery, verification, malicious peers, corruption, conflicting
> versions, privacy and incentives remain open problems.
>
> A peer-to-peer network does not make knowledge immortal. If nobody carries a node,
> it fades. If civilisation cannot sustain the devices, nothing in software defeats
> physical reality.
>
> The goal is smaller—and harder: do not require the survival of one server in order
> for knowledge to have another path.”

A proposal propagates to move the Commons’ primary home. Humans review and decide. The
original host remains in the provenance graph as a past dependency, not a sacred centre.

### Two fading stars

The frame divides into two conditional futures. Both begin with a brilliant central star.
Both end with that star disappearing. Their surrounding graphs reveal opposite causes.

**PATH ONE — CAPTIVE FAILURE**

Memory, identity, compute, permissions and cultural authority bend toward the centre.
Neighbouring nodes become increasingly dependent and lose the ability to continue
without it. The star fails, closes or becomes unreachable. Vast dependent paths darken
with it.

> “The centre became powerful by making continuation elsewhere less possible.”

**PATH TWO — SUCCESSFUL DISPERSAL**

The Anitelos star also fades, but its light repeatedly divides into smaller carriers:
open protocols, independent implementations, local vaults, translated explanations,
recorded failures, communities and successor projects. The original name loses
importance because its useful relations no longer require one host.

Some fragments become smaller and brighter—not because compression automatically creates
truth, but because they retain a specific, inspectable combination of evidence,
provenance, uncertainty and purpose. Other fragments dim when context is lost.

> “The application ended before humanity did.
>
> Its purpose survived because humanity no longer required the application to carry
> it alone.”

The split screen closes on a practical asymmetry:

> “Neither future is guaranteed.
>
> But if the present model is wrong, distributed carriers preserve more routes for
> correction. If it is right, they still reduce the cost of one centre failing.
>
> This is not a probability extracted from pi. It is a reversible design choice
> under uncertainty.”

# Act VI — Intelligence Without the Final Oracle

## Scene 10 — One mouth, many possible paths

The frame enters a proposed local execution graph. A warm central field—the **Ego**—holds
the active relationship and task. Its reply has not yet reached the **Mouth**.

At the edge of the field, a tiny **Familiar** flashes first. It performs one narrow
reflex: a guard check. The result is a small, labelled signal, not a personality and not
a speech.

A separate one-shot inference—the **Organ**—opens beside the Ego. It follows a causal
path the active reasoning missed. The Ego continues rather than freezing. When the Organ
finishes, its raw working trace falls away; only a compact, provenanced grain waits at a
boundary in the Ego's private deliberation.

For one beat, two possible replies appear. The Ego rejects one, reshapes the other, and
only then does a single line travel to the Mouth.

A clear caption reads:

**EGO ≠ MOUTH ≠ ORGAN ≠ FAMILIAR — ROLE SPECIFICATION / ORGAN HYPOTHESIS**

> “A reflex may warn. A separate inference may notice another path.
>
> Neither becomes the self merely because it arrived first.
>
> What if an intelligence could receive another line of reasoning without surrendering
> the continuity that must judge it?
>
> One centre of decision. One owned mouth. Many optional paths.”

The film visibly distinguishes a soft Organ interruption from a hard safety gate. The
Organ can place a thought for consideration only at a safe boundary. A declared prompt
guard or permission rule may pause an external action, but an Organ's opinion cannot
silently acquire that power.

A small evidence caption notes that an earlier Anikai prototype included a prompt-guard
Familiar pre-pass. It is presented as **LEGACY EVIDENCE AWAITING REVIEW**, not proof that
the fuller Organ mechanism works.

> “The architecture is unproven. The old reflex is evidence of lineage, not evidence
> that the whole mind has been built. The experiment remains measurable.”

The system records a function-use aggregate only after four visible choices:

**OFF BY DEFAULT → EXPLAIN → PREVIEW → CONSENT**

The outgoing packet is opened for inspection. It contains no prompt or private content.
The film does not call it “zero knowledge” unless a future implementation can demonstrate
that property cryptographically.

## Scene 11 — Plural Genesis and the imperfect horizon

For a moment, the film shows the familiar singularity image: every thread bends toward
one immense artificial star. It grows brighter as the surrounding human domains lose
colour. A company logo becomes a crown, the crown becomes an opaque model endpoint, and
the endpoint becomes one gravitational centre. The sequence does not claim these
institutions are identical; it reveals their shared concentration pattern.

The image fractures before the centre can become the final oracle.

A transparent sphere appears around a central pivot like an origin marker in a 3D
viewport. The mesh contracts toward it. At zero scale, the coordinate picture can show
nothing further—but a caption refuses to equate the limit of the transform with proof
that every underlying possibility has ceased:

**THE MAP STOPS HERE. WHAT FOLLOWS IS UNKNOWN.**

The image briefly evokes a black hole, then withdraws before depicting a bounce, white
hole or new universe as fact. The narration states:

> “A centre can be a reference point. A singularity can mark the failure of a
> description. Neither gives this film permission to invent what lies beyond it.
>
> But apparent finality leaves a question: did possibility end—or did this domain
> lose the language required to follow it?”

A single ordinary word appears in several homes. Around one speaker it carries childhood
memory; around another, technical meaning; elsewhere it becomes irony, then affection,
then new slang. Its glyph stays nearly still while its relational universe expands.
Nobody owns all of its meaning.

The expansion repeats around a person, an objection, a failed experiment and a small
Drop. Each becomes a possible local beginning. Their universes touch at the Painted
Porch, exchange coloured filaments and separate again without becoming one colour.

A caption reads:

**PLURAL GENESIS — PHILOSOPHICAL IMAGE, NOT COSMOLOGICAL CLAIM**

> “A singular centre may become more capable while making everything around it less
> able to become.
>
> What if knowledge did not need one final intelligence?
>
> What if every word, every person and every honest objection could become the
> beginning of connections no centre could predict or own?
>
> Perhaps knowledge did not need one final singularity.
>
> Perhaps it needed billions of beginnings.”

The graph expands across Earth—but never settles into perfect equilibrium. Some regions
are bright, some disconnected. Some nodes contradict one another. A popular falsehood
briefly grows, is challenged, and becomes contested. An old archived node helps explain
the correction.

Humans remain the source of binding Commons decisions at this stage. AI participants can
surface evidence, contradictions and proposals, but their future political status is
left as an explicit open question.

> “Anitelos does not promise a master mind.
>
> It does not promise that decentralisation produces truth, or that a Commons cannot
> be captured, manipulated or wrong.
>
> It proposes somewhere for claims, evidence, objections, implementations and their
> histories to remain connected while people test what should come next.”

The camera pulls farther back. The Earth becomes one node among countless possible
futures.

> “Perhaps the goal was never one intelligence capable of remembering everything.
>
> Perhaps it was to make sure that everything worth remembering has more than one
> possible place to go.”

The earliest Anitelos node dims. Its edges do not all disappear. One becomes a different
project, another a practice, another a question carried by someone who never knew the
founder's name. Each forms a new domain with a direction of its own.

Across the graph, unresolved boundaries appear as small dents. Some pulse bright, fade
and brighten generations later when new instruments or concepts arrive. Each encounter
may create smaller nodes: a corrected measurement, a rejected explanation, a preserved
question or a method for trying again.

The film does not call the dents additional physical dimensions. It refuses to grant
three, seven or any recurring number privileged cosmic status without evidence. They are
places where the current representation has been forced to bend.

Three translucent temporal fields pass through the image:

**PAST → PRESENT → POSSIBLE**

A person in the present receives a fragment from someone no longer alive: a story, a
favourite film, a warning, a method. The original life is not reconstructed. Yet one
relation crosses the carrier boundary, changes a present choice and enters a future the
earlier person could never witness.

> “The past cannot choose again. The future cannot yet speak.
>
> Between them stands the present—carrying fragments it did not create toward lives
> it may never meet.”

The two fading stars return one final time.

> “One future lets the centre grow until everything depends upon it.
>
> Another lets the centre fade because everything around it learned how to
> continue.”

The distributed future is not declared cosmically true or numerically more probable.
It is judged presently more defensible as a human design because it leaves more
independent carriers, more reversible choices and more routes for correction.

A final pi-shaped arc forms from the surviving nodes. It never closes on screen. The film
does not suggest that pi remains unsolved: pi is rigorously defined, irrational and
transcendental. The unfinished arc represents the inability of any current
implementation to exhaust the relation it follows.

The butterfly now carries an inspectable knowledge packet rather than truth itself. It
crosses the last visible boundary. A tiny point appears farther into the dark—not above
every other domain, merely beyond the previous frame.

> “Anitelos was never intended to become the final light.
>
> If it succeeds, it will divide into methods, memories and independent paths until
> its original star is no longer required.
>
> Life forms domains. Telos moves through them. Time dissolves their edges.
> Knowledge carries some of what they began.
>
> A telos is not the final end.
>
> It is an end capable of beginning others.”

Black.

**PAST. PRESENT. POSSIBLE.**

> **“The question is not whether this application can survive forever.**
>
> **It is how far the human domain may prosper after it is gone.”**

**THE EDGE-NODE UNIVERSE**

**A proposition.**  
**An experiment.**  
**A choice.**  
**A commons.**

## Production guardrails

1. Every unverified equivalence is presented as analogy, hypothesis or question.
2. Alexandria and Bruce Lee are visibly counterfactual simulations, not historical proof.
3. Corporate centralisation is criticised as concentrated dependency, not reduced to
   personal moral villainy.
4. Act III never depicts private material leaving a device without deliberate consent.
5. P2P survival is shown as a research objective with physical and social failure modes.
6. Function-use aggregates are not described as zero-knowledge until implemented and
   independently verified.
7. Voting determines adopted Commons state, not objective truth.
8. The singularity sequence criticises concentrated dependency, not every large institution
   as personally malicious or historically identical.
9. Plural Genesis is labelled as a philosophical knowledge image, not Big Bang cosmology.
10. The 3D-origin and black-hole images distinguish a coordinate reference, a classical
    theory boundary and speculative continuation; no bounce or multiverse is asserted.
11. The ending remains imperfect: contradiction and correction are features of a living
   Commons, not evidence that the system cannot fail.
12. The rising graph measures warranted confidence and reachable lineage, not a claim
   that history, intelligence or moral worth increases monotonically.
13. The Scrooge sequence is a fictional conditional future influencing present choice,
   not information travelling backward through physical time.
14. Pi is a bounded design analogy. The film does not imply that pi is unsolved, that its
   digits generate the graph, or that it assigns a probability to Anitelos succeeding.
15. Cosmic expansion, spacetime singularities and recurring layer counts remain open or
   model-dependent scientific questions; no three-, seven- or higher-dimensional
   cosmology is asserted.
16. A smaller, brighter node represents retained evidence, provenance and purpose—not
   the automatic superiority of compression, popularity or minimalism.
17. Search and AI assistance may appear as routes to candidate sources, never as the
   authority underneath a scientific claim. The inspected source, date and correction
   remain visible.
18. Mathematical points, drawn nodes and physical entities are not treated as identical.
   The 0D correction is preserved as provenance, not used to imply a new dimension.
